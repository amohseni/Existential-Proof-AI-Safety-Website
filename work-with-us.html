Working with us

We want to hear from students and early-career researchers who would like to work on these questions. Prospective graduate students can apply to the philosophy PhD programs at UNC and Carnegie Mellon. Researchers elsewhere can work with us through the fellowships listed below. Write to either of us with a short note on what you would like to work on and which of our key questions it bears on. Addresses are below.

Mentoring

Daniel Herrmann advises PhD students at UNC, mentors AI safety researchers through the PIBBSS Fellowship, the Iliad Fellowship, and the Algoverse AI Safety Fellowship, and teaches AI Interpretability and AI and the Future of Humanity at UNC. Aydin Mohseni leads SPAR research teams, advises student theses at Carnegie Mellon, teaches Game Theory, AI and X-Risk and Decision Theory there, and teaches decision theory and agent foundations at summer schools in Pittsburgh.

Current and past students and mentees are listed on the People page.

Key questions

Questions we would like help with, by research area.

Agency

  1. When does a decision theory describe a trained system well enough to predict how its behavior generalizes?
  2. Can models that are causally isolated from one another still coordinate?
  3. What is a world model, and what does a causal model do for the agent that has one?

Interpretability

  1. What would make a probe for deception or situational awareness a measurement of it?
  2. What does an evaluation show when the system can tell that it is being evaluated?
  3. Probes are validated against behavior and then used to check behavioral evaluations. Where does that regress stop?

Value

  1. Which aggregation rules do current training pipelines assume, and how should they be compared?
  2. How should a system act for one person who is uncertain about her own preferences, when expected preference is not well defined?

Alignment

  1. How reliable must the measurement of a trait be for a given deployment decision?
  2. What is it for an agent to allow itself to be changed, as opposed to merely being changeable?
  3. Can a system share our values while reasoning by a different decision theory?
