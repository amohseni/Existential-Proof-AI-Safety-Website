Evolutionary Theory of Advanced AI Adaptations

Active · Aydin Mohseni, Daniel Herrmann, Sven Neth

Which pressures shape the behavioral dispositions of AI systems as they are trained and deployed, and how do those pressures bear on alignment?

Claims that capable AI systems will seek power, resist shutdown, or deceive their overseers are usually stated loosely. We treat each such claim of instrumental convergenceInstrumental convergence: the thesis that agents with very different final goals will pursue similar intermediate goals, such as acquiring resources, because those goals help with almost any final goal. as a claim with six parts. Writing the parts out shows which versions of a claim are theorems, which are empirical conjectures, and which are not yet well defined.

Current work: the power-seeking theorems

Turner and colleagues proved that, for most reward functions, optimal policies tend to seek power.A. M. Turner, L. Smith, R. Shah, A. Critch, and P. Tadepalli, “Optimal Policies Tend to Seek Power,” NeurIPS 2021; A. M. Turner and P. Tadepalli, “Parametrically Retargetable Decision-Makers Tend to Seek Power,” NeurIPS 2022. The proofs count reward functions by a symmetry argument that many readers find hard to interpret. We construct the prior that the counting assumes, which turns each result into a statement about probability. The probabilities then admit two interpretations.

OutputsAn Epistemic Reading of the Power-Seeking Theorems

AI Alignment as a Principal–Agent Problem

Status · Aydin Mohseni

Formal models of delegation, incentives, and misalignment.

Why it mattersEconomics has a mature theory of delegation to agents whose actions the principal cannot fully observe. That theory gives exact conditions under which delegation goes wrong, and those conditions can be checked against AI systems.
