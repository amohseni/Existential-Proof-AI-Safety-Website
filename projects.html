Evolutionary Theory of Advanced AI Adaptations

Active · Aydin Mohseni, Daniel Herrmann, Sven Neth

Which pressures shape the behavioral dispositions of AI systems as they are trained and deployed, and how do those pressures bear on alignment?

Claims that capable AI systems will seek power, resist shutdown, or deceive their overseers are usually stated loosely. We treat each such claim of instrumental convergenceInstrumental convergence: the thesis that agents with very different final goals will pursue similar intermediate goals, such as acquiring resources, because those goals help with almost any final goal. as a claim with six parts:

Systems of kind A, trained by processes T, on tasks and data distributed as D, converge in sense C to behavior pattern B in situations S.

The behavior patterns B in view include coherence, coordination, collusion, deception, learning, situational awareness, proxy gaming, power-seeking, resource acquisition, and self-preservation. Current work concerns power-seeking.

Current work: the power-seeking theorems

Turner and colleagues proved that, for most reward functions, optimal policies tend to seek power.A. M. Turner, L. Smith, R. Shah, A. Critch, and P. Tadepalli, “Optimal Policies Tend to Seek Power,” NeurIPS 2021; A. M. Turner and P. Tadepalli, “Parametrically Retargetable Decision-Makers Tend to Seek Power,” NeurIPS 2022. The proofs count reward functions by a symmetry argument that many readers find hard to interpret. We construct the prior that the counting assumes, which turns each result into a statement about probability. The probabilities then admit two interpretations.

Radical AI Interpretability

Preprint, 2026 · Daniel A. Herrmann, Benjamin A. Levinstein

How can we tell what an AI system believes and wants from facts about its computation?

Interpretability methods read beliefs and goals off a model’s internal states. Whether a reading is correct depends on what it is for a state to be a belief, and the methods leave this unstated. We take up the problem of radical interpretation,Radical interpretation: the problem, posed by Davidson and Lewis, of working out what an agent believes, wants, and means from facts that do not presuppose any of these. with the facts about the agent given by its computation:

Find the beliefs B, desires D, and meanings M that together best explain the computation C of system S.

The three are solved for together. Belief, desire, and the structure of the propositions they concern constrain one another, so a method that holds one fixed while measuring another inherits its distortions.

Current work: from belief to safety-relevant quantities

Earlier papers found that leading probes for belief in language models fail to generalize,B. A. Levinstein and D. A. Herrmann, “Still No Lie Detector for Language Models: Probing Empirical and Conceptual Roadblocks,” Philosophical Studies 182 (2025). and proposed four conditions that a representation must meet to count as belief-like.D. A. Herrmann and B. A. Levinstein, “Standards for Belief Representations in LLMs,” Minds and Machines 35 (2025). The book ties criteria of success to tests that current methods can run. The next step is to give deception and situational awareness the same treatment.

A Bayesian Reduction of Causation

Daniel A. Herrmann, Benjamin A. Levinstein, Aydin Mohseni, Bruce Rushing

Does causal reasoning need anything beyond subjective probability?

Pearl’s causal hierarchy separates associational, interventional, and counterfactual claims, and theorems in this tradition show that the higher kinds cannot be recovered from the lower.J. Pearl, Causality, Cambridge University Press, 2000; E. Bareinboim, J. D. Correa, D. Ibeling, and T. Icard, “On Pearl’s Hierarchy and the Foundations of Causal Inference,” 2022. We argue that these results rest on an impoverished set of variables. Add a variable for each intervention, treated as an event in the world like any other, and interventional claims become conditional probabilities:

P(Y = y | do(X = x)) = P(Y = y | X = x, IX = on).

A representation theorem in the style of de Finetti then shows how an agent can learn causal structure and probabilities together. For decision theory, causal decision theory becomes a useful approximation to evidential decision theory, and causalist intuitions trace to the limits of the reasoner.

What it means for world models

To say that a trained system has a world model can mean that it encodes the structure of its environment, or that its model supports reasoning about interventions. The second matters more for safety, since a system that can reason about interventions can plan around its overseers. If causal structure is what a bounded agent’s picture of the world looks like when it attends to only part of what it could track, a system may reason well about interventions with no explicit causal machinery inside it. Interpretability should look for that capacity in whatever form it takes.

AI Alignment as a Principal–Agent Problem

Daniel A. Herrmann, Benjamin A. Levinstein

When is imperfect alignment good enough to delegate a decision to an AI system?

Perfect alignment is unlikely, so we need to know when a system that does not fully share our values is good enough to hand a task. We model the choice as a decision for a principal who is uncertain about the system’s values and abilities, and describe the system only by how it would choose.In practice we observe what a system does, not what it believes or wants. None of the results assume that the system maximizes expected utility. Delegating the problems she expects it to face pays when

reach + information > cost of misalignment.

Reach is the value of the problems the system faces that she would not, information is what its choices reveal about the world, and the cost of misalignment is what its departures from her own choices cost her.

Current work: knife-edges and margins

Dorst and colleagues propose that to defer to an expert is to prefer her opinions for any decision whatever.K. Dorst, B. A. Levinstein, B. Salow, B. E. Husic, and B. Fitelson, “Deference Done Better,” Philosophical Perspectives 35 (2021). Carried over to delegation, where the agent’s values come along with its opinions, this standard becomes a knife-edge. The ex ante standard is robust. A system that errs only on close calls, within δ of her indifference, costs at most Kδ², where K measures how often she faces close calls, while its information does not shrink with δ.

Two projects run through SPAR (Supervised Program for Alignment Research) in fall 2026, each led by Aydin Mohseni with two team members.

The Robustness of Agential Representations

Active, fall 2026 · Aydin Mohseni with Jens Jäger and Haotian Zong

When a language model's choices are interpreted as expected-utility maximization, how coherent are the credences and utilities attributed to it, and how robust is that attribution?

The project tests the attribution in four ways: whether it predicts the model's choices in and out of sample; whether steering the putative credence and utility representations shifts choices as expected-utility theory predicts; whether it agrees with credences and utilities decoded by probes; and whether it is stable under rephrasings that preserve meaning.

Bayes-Optimal Research Protocols for Automated AI Safety Research

Active, fall 2026 · Aydin Mohseni with Yosra Kazemi and Yang Hu

How should compute be allocated across research directions when AI systems do the research?

The project studies Bayes-optimal allocation of compute across research directions in AI theorem proving, with results verified in Lean.
