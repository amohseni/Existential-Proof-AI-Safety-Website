AI Alignment and Delegation

When should we (not) delegate decisions to an AI system, if it may not share our values?

Overview

When is a system that does not share our values still worth delegating to?

Perfect value alignment of an AI system is unlikely, so we need to know when a system that does not fully share our values is good enough to deploy. We call this the question of alignment sufficiency. We consider contexts in which we are uncertain about various traits of the AI, including its beliefs, goals, and behavior.Ultimately our analysis bottoms out in the behavior of the agent. Sometimes certain patterns in this behavior are conveniently summarized with the language of belief and goals. We develop precise ways to think about making deployment decisions in these contexts.

Why it matters

We want to provide a rigorous way to think about building safety cases. A safety case is the argument a lab makes that a system is safe enough to deploy. We want to formalize the optimal way one should make a deployment decision so that we can have clear standards of success and failure for safety cases. Our decision-theoretic approach articulates what the lab would need to demonstrate about its model in order to justify deployment.

Current work

Margins of Misalignment and Principal-Agent Problems

A first strand of active research, led by Daniel Herrmann and Ben Levinstein, develops a general decision-theoretic framework for making deployment decisions. The framework models our uncertainty about the behavior of a system: it doesn't assume that the system is representable as an agent. Even in these very general settings we can show that rational deployment depends on whether or not the gains from behavioral analogues of how much a system knows and how capable it is outweigh the cost of its possible misalignment.

A second strand, led by Aydin Mohseni and L. G. Demo, treats alignment as a principal-agent problem, with formal models of delegation, incentives, and misalignment between a principal and a more capable agent.
