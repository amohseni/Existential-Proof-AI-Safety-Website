Research

Research areas and programs

We study AI systems as agents. Our work falls into four areas: when a system can usefully be described as an agent, what it believes and wants, how it should choose when values are uncertain, and when imperfect alignment is good enough to delegate a decision to it.

Areas

Agency

When AI systems can be modeled as agents with beliefs, preferences, and ways of choosing, and what such models predict.

Whether and when we can profitably describe AI systems using the vocabulary and theoretical machinery of agency informs many approaches to AI safety.

Key questions

  1. When does a decision theory describe a trained system well enough to predict how its behavior generalizes?
  2. Can models that are causally isolated from one another still coordinate?
  3. What is a world model, and what does a causal model do for the agent that has one?

Programs

  • Evolutionary Theory of Advanced AI AdaptationsThe pressures that shape the behavioral dispositions of advanced AI systems, starting with power-seeking.
  • Naturalizing AgencyRecovering causation and control from an agent’s own beliefs and desires.

Interpretability

How to tell what an AI system believes and wants, and when a probe or evaluation measures it.

Understanding the internal structure of AI systems, and how that structure produces their outputs, can help us both make deployment decisions and guide monitoring and intervention efforts. We aim to provide practice-informed and conceptually rigorous foundations of interpretability.

Key questions

  1. What would make a probe for deception or situational awareness a measurement of it?
  2. What does an evaluation show when the system can tell that it is being evaluated?
  3. Probes are validated against behavior and then used to check behavioral evaluations. Where does that regress stop?

Program

  • Measuring Beliefs and Desires in AI SystemsCriteria for attributing beliefs and desires to AI systems, tested on trained models from small transformers to language models.

Value

How to choose when values are uncertain: one’s own, or those of the people one acts for.

Decisions about how to train and deploy AI systems both explicitly and implicitly encode certain values into the systems. We want to make this process more explicit and provide guidance on how to make difficult value choices under conditions of uncertainty.

Key questions

  1. Which aggregation rules do current training pipelines assume, and how should they be compared?
  2. How should a system act for one person who is uncertain about her own preferences, when expected preference is not well defined?

Program

  • Decision under Value UncertaintyLearning what one values, and acting for someone whose preferences one does not know.

Alignment

When an AI system whose values differ from ours is good enough to delegate a decision to.

Perfect alignment is unlikely, so the practical question is when imperfect alignment is good enough. We aim to develop a calculus of misalignment and capability trade-offs that can guide deployment decisions.

Key questions

  1. How reliable must the measurement of a trait be for a given deployment decision?
  2. What is it for an agent to allow itself to be changed, as opposed to merely being changeable?
  3. Can a system share our values while reasoning by a different decision theory?

Program

  • AI Alignment and DelegationA calculus of delegation: how much misalignment we can tolerate, given our beliefs about the system’s goals and capabilities. Everything bottoms out in the behavior of the system and the consequences for us.
