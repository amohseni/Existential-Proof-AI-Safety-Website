Research

Research areas and programs

We study AI systems as agents. Our work falls into four areas: when a system can usefully be described as an agent, what interpretability methods and evaluations tell us about it, how it should choose when values are uncertain, and when imperfect alignment is good enough to delegate a decision to it.

Areas

Agency

When AI systems can be modeled as agents with beliefs, preferences, and ways of choosing, and what such models predict.

Whether and when we can profitably describe AI systems using the vocabulary and theoretical machinery of agency informs many approaches to AI safety.

Example questions

  1. Under what assumptions about a system’s goals and its environment does power-seeking follow from pursuing almost any goal, and which of those assumptions, dropped, make it fail?
  2. If we cut the channels two copies of a model use to communicate, can they still coordinate? Causal and evidential decision theories predict different answers.
  3. What would show that a system treats an outcome as within its control, and can we read that off its beliefs and desires?

Programs

  • Evolutionary Theory of Advanced AI AdaptationsThe pressures that shape the behavioral dispositions of advanced AI systems, starting with power-seeking.
  • Naturalizing AgencyRecovering causation and control from an agent’s own beliefs and desires.

Interpretability

What interpretability methods and evaluations measure, and what their results license us to conclude about a system.

Understanding the internal structure of AI systems, and how that structure produces their outputs, can help us both make deployment decisions and guide monitoring and intervention efforts. We aim to provide practice-informed and conceptually rigorous foundations of interpretability.

Example questions

  1. A model fails an eval for a dangerous capability. What licenses the conclusion that it lacks the capability, especially if it can tell it is being tested?
  2. What would make a probe for deception a measurement of deception, and not of something that merely goes with deception in the probe’s training data?
  3. Probes are trained on our sentences. How do we find the propositions a model’s own states are about, when they need not line up with ours?

Program

  • Measuring Beliefs and Desires in AI SystemsCriteria for attributing beliefs and desires to AI systems, tested on small transformers and on language models.

Value

How to choose when one is uncertain about one’s own values, or about the values of the people one acts for.

Decisions about how to train and deploy AI systems build values into them, sometimes explicitly and often implicitly. We want to make this process more explicit, and to give guidance on making hard value choices under uncertainty.

Example questions

  1. Reinforcement learning from human feedback (RLHF) and model constitutions already settle whose feedback counts and how conflicts are resolved. Which aggregation rules do they assume, and how should those rules be compared?
  2. How should AI systems make decisions on our behalf when they are uncertain of our preferences?
  3. How should an AI system aggregate several dimensions of value when they are difficult to compare?

Program

  • Decision under Value UncertaintyLearning what one values, and acting for someone whose preferences one does not know.

Alignment

When an AI system whose values differ from ours is good enough to delegate a decision to.

Perfect alignment is unlikely, so the practical question is when imperfect alignment is good enough. We aim to develop a calculus of delegation under possible misalignment that can guide deployment decisions.

Example questions

  1. It is difficult to specify exactly what we care about, so we often use proxies. These tend to work only under certain conditions. How far outside those conditions can a system optimize a proxy before the person it acts for is worse off?
  2. Can a system share our values while reasoning by a different decision theory?
  3. How reliable must the measurement of a trait be for a given deployment decision?

Program

  • AI Alignment and DelegationA calculus of delegation: how much misalignment we can tolerate, given our beliefs about the system’s goals and capabilities. Everything bottoms out in the behavior of the system and the consequences for us.
