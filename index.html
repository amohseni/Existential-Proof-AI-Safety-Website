Mission

We are searching for a path to safe AI, if one exists.

We use decision and game theory, mechanistic interpretability, and philosophical analysis to understand advanced AI systems as agents, and to search for principled routes to interpretability, alignment, and safety.

Aydin Mohseni · Carnegie Mellon University
Daniel Herrmann · University of North Carolina at Chapel Hill

Research

Agency

How should we model artificial systems as agents with beliefs, preferences, and decision procedures?

Interpretability

What do interpretability methods and evaluations measure, and what do their results license us to conclude?

Value

How should a system choose when it is unsure what it values, or what the people it acts for value?

Alignment

How can we safely delegate to increasingly capable systems whose objectives may differ from ours?

Programs

  • Evolutionary Theory of Advanced AI AdaptationsThe pressures that shape the behavioral dispositions of advanced AI systems, starting with power-seeking.
  • Naturalizing AgencyRecovering causation and control from an agent’s own beliefs and desires.
  • Measuring Beliefs and Desires in AI SystemsCriteria for attributing beliefs and desires to AI systems, tested on small transformers and on language models.
  • Decision under Value UncertaintyLearning what one values, and acting for someone whose preferences one does not know.
  • AI Alignment and DelegationA calculus of delegation: how much misalignment we can tolerate, given our beliefs about the system’s goals and capabilities. Everything bottoms out in the behavior of the system and the consequences for us.
