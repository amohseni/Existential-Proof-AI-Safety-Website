AI safety research

We are searching for a path to safe AI, if one exists.

We use decision and game theory, mechanistic interpretability, and philosophical analysis to understand advanced AI systems as agents, and to search for principled routes to interpretability, alignment, and safety.

Aydin Mohseni · Carnegie Mellon University
Daniel Herrmann · University of North Carolina at Chapel Hill

Research

Agency

How should we model artificial systems as agents with beliefs, preferences, and decision procedures?

Interpretability

What evidence warrants attributing beliefs, goals, or representations to advanced AI systems?

Value

How should we think about value uncertainty, preference, and normative learning?

Alignment

How can humans safely delegate to increasingly capable systems whose objectives may diverge from their own?

Programs

  • Evolutionary Theory of Advanced AI AdaptationsThe pressures that shape the behavioral dispositions of advanced AI systems, starting with power-seeking.
  • Naturalizing AgencyRecovering causation and control from an agent’s own beliefs and desires.
  • Measuring Beliefs and Desires in AI SystemsWhen an AI system has beliefs and goals, and how we could measure them.
  • Decision under Value UncertaintyChoosing when one’s own values, or those of the person one acts for, are uncertain.
  • AI Alignment as a Principal–Agent ProblemWhen imperfect alignment is good enough to delegate a decision to an AI system.
